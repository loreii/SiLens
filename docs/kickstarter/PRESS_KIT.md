### SiLens Launches Kickstarter for World's First Open-Source Hardwired Vision AI Accelerator

*Revolutionary PCIe card delivers 100× faster AI inference than consumer GPUs at a fraction of the power*

**[CITY], [DATE]** — SiLens Technologies today announced the Kickstarter launch of SiLens, a groundbreaking AI accelerator that permanently etches a vision-language neural network into silicon. The PCIe card delivers sub-5ms inference latency — over 100× faster than a $300 consumer GPU — while consuming just 25 watts of power.

"The AI hardware industry has a dirty secret," said [Founder Name], CEO of SiLens Technologies. "Modern GPUs waste 95% of their capability when running small models. We asked: what if we built hardware that does one thing perfectly instead of everything poorly?"

**How It Works**

Traditional AI accelerators store model weights in memory and load them repeatedly during inference. SiLens takes a radical approach: each of the 246 million weights in SmolVLM-256M — a state-of-the-art vision-language model — is encoded as a physical wire connection in the chip.

The result: computation happens at the speed of electrical propagation, not memory access.

SiLens will release its complete design under Apache 2.0 license, including RTL source code, PCB schematics, and drivers. The company is using SkyWater's open-source SKY130 process design kit, making this the first fully open AI accelerator.

"We believe AI hardware should be as open as AI software," said [Founder Name]. "Anyone should be able to understand, modify, and build upon our work."

| Form factor | Full-size GPU | Half-height PCIe | Fits anywhere |
| Open source | No | Yes | Fully open |

### Why the Difference?

GPUs are memory-bandwidth limited for small model inference. The RTX 4060's 288 GB/s memory bandwidth becomes the bottleneck, not its 18 TFLOPS of compute.

SiLens eliminates memory access entirely — weights are hardwired circuits, not stored data.

## FAQ for Press

**Q: Is this vaporware?**
A: No. We have working RTL simulations, verified designs, and relationships with SkyWater Technology. This campaign funds tape-out and production.

**Q: Why 130nm? That's ancient.**
A: It's the only fully open-source PDK available. We prioritize openness over bleeding-edge specs. Future versions will use advanced nodes as open PDKs mature.

**Q: Can it run other models?**
A: No. Weights are physically fixed. This is a trade-off: infinite speed-up for the hardwired model, zero flexibility.

**Q: Who's the competition?**
A: No one does exactly what we do. Google Coral and Intel Movidius are vision-only. GPUs are general-purpose. We're the first hardwired vision-language accelerator.

**Q: What's the business model?**
A: Hardware sales plus future enterprise support/licensing. Open source doesn't mean we can't build a business — see Red Hat, Canonical, etc.
